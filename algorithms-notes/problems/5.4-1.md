How many people must there be before the probability that at least two people have a birthday on July 4 is greater than $1/2$ ?

### Part 2

Let $$ X = '\text{Number of people whose birthday is on DD/YYYY}'$$ Then:

$$
\begin{align} 
 P(X \geq 2) &= 1 - P(X < 2) \\ 
&= 1 - (P(X = 1) + P(X = 0)) \\ 
&= 1 - P(X = 1) - P(X = 0)
\end{align}

$$

We can approach this problem by two ways (Binomial and Poisson)

##### Binomial
if $X \sim \mathscr{B}(n,p)$  
$$
P(X = x) = \binom{n}{x}p^x(1-p)^{n-x}
$$
For this problem $n = \text{number of people}$ and $p=\frac{1}{365}$
$$
P(X = x) = \binom{n}{x} \left(\frac{1}{365}\right)^x \left(\frac{364}{365}\right)^{n-x}
$$

##### Poisson
if $X \sim \mathscr{P}(\lambda)$  
$$
P(X = x) = \frac{e^{-\lambda} \lambda^{x}}{x!}
$$
For this problem $\lambda = \frac{n}{365}$
$$
 P(X= x) = \frac{e^{-\frac{n}{365}}\frac{n}{365}^{x}}{x!}
$$
if $X = 1$
$$
P(X=1)=e^{-\frac{n}{365}}\frac{n}{365}
$$
if $X = 0$
$$
P(X=1)=e^{-\frac{n}{365}}
$$
Replacing 
$$
\begin{align}
P(X \geq 2) &= 1 - P(X = 1) - P(X = 0) \\
&= 1 - e^{-\frac{n}{365}}\frac{n}{365} - e^{-\frac{n}{365}} \\
&= 1 - e^{-\frac{n}{365}} \left( \frac{n}{365} + 1 \right) \\
&= 1 - e^{-\frac{n}{365}} \left( \frac{n + 365}{365}\right)
\end{align}
$$
